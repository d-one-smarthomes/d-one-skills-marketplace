# D-One Lead Filter

Decide whether a lead is worth D-One's time — and do it quickly. The point of this framework is
discipline: D-One's biggest competitive advantage is not saying yes to more work, it's saying no to
the *wrong* work. Reserve D-One's reputation and leadership attention for jobs that build the business
Darren actually wants to own. Hold the power seat — D-One does not beg for work.

---

## Step 1 — Deduce first, ask second

Before asking Darren anything, extract everything already knowable from the enquiry, the forwarded
email, the attached drawings/quote, Gmail history with this contact, and Asana. Only ask about the
gates you genuinely can't deduce. Never ask a question the context already answers.

Often deducible without asking: rough project scale (a Val de Vie / Winelands estate villa vs a small
flat), location, whether it's a builder/architect D-One already knows, and often whether the client is
price-shopping (multiple quotes requested, "cheapest option", pushing for discounts).

---

## Step 2 — Run the six gates (ordered, short-circuit)

Work through the gates **in order** — highest-impact first, so no time is wasted on a lead that was
never a fit. **The instant a deal-breaker is confirmed, stop and go to the decline step — don't ask
the rest.** Ask only for the unknown gates, and batch them into a single set of tappable options
(single-select) so Darren answers in a few taps. Keep it to eight questions at most — though scope,
value and origin are often obvious from the enquiry itself, so most leads need far fewer taps.

| # | Gate | Ask (if unknown) | Deal-breaker trigger |
|---|------|------------------|----------------------|
| 1 | **Origin** — international or local? D-One has far more success with international clients than local ones, so this is a strong predictor. | Is this an international or a local client? `International` / `Local` | `Local` is a meaningful negative weight — and a hard decline if the "local decline" rule is switched on (see below) |
| 2 | **Scope** — does it include audio / audio-visual **and** system integration, not just the basics? | Does the project include AV and system integration, or is it basics-only? `AV + integration` / `Basics only (CCTV / access / Wi-Fi)` | **Basics only** → decline. D-One does not take on security-only, access-control-only or networking-only projects. We only pursue jobs that include audio/AV and system integration (plus the basics as part of the whole). |
| 3 | **On TV?** — is this a project we'd proudly showcase, the kind D-One wants to be known for? | Would this project be on TV? `Yes` / `Maybe` / `No` | `No` is a strong lean to decline (off-brand) |
| 4 | **Value** — is it > R1M? | Estimated project value? `<R1M` / `R1M–R4M` / `>R4M` | <R1M isn't a hard no, but weakens the case |
| 5 | **Product control** — can we specify the core kit? | Do we get to choose/specify the core equipment? `We specify` / `Partly` / `Locked to someone else's spec` | **Locked out** → decline |
| 6 | **Margin** — is it 30% or better? | Realistic gross margin? `≥30%` / `<30%` | **Below 30%** → decline |
| 7 | **Geography** — in our area, or is the travel worth it? | Location? `In our area` / `Travel, but worth it` / `Too far to justify` | **Too far to justify** → decline |
| 8 | **Client** — will this be good to work with? | Value-buyer (not price-shopping), decisive, realistic, respects our expertise, and we're talking to the actual decision-maker? `Green` / `Some flags` / `Red flags` | **Insists on lowest price**, or red flags (refuses our standard process, untrustworthy, unrealistic) → decline |

**Scope is a hard filter.** A lead that only wants CCTV, access control or Wi-Fi/networking — with no
audio/AV and no system integration — is an automatic decline, regardless of value or margin. Those aren't
the projects D-One wants. AV + integration must be part of the job for it to qualify.

**International vs local (the "local decline" rule):** By default, `Local` is a strong negative weight in the
Strategic score, not an automatic no — D-One still wins good local work and a large chunk of the current
pipeline is local Winelands projects. If Darren decides to go international-only, flip this into a hard
deal-breaker (any local client → decline) by adding "Local client" to the deal-breaker list below. Until
then, treat a local lead as needing to be *clearly* strong on the other gates to proceed.

Capacity is **not** a gate — D-One subcontracts when necessary, so a full calendar rarely kills a good
job. Treat capacity as a delivery note in scoring, not a hard filter.

### Absolute deal-breakers (any one = decline, regardless of score)

- Basics-only scope — CCTV / access control / networking only, no audio/AV and no system integration
- Gross margin below 30%
- We can't specify the core equipment
- Client insists on the lowest price / buys on price, not value
- Outside the service area and travel isn't worth it
- Unacceptable payment terms
- Client refuses D-One's standard process
- Excessive technical or contractual risk

## Step 3 — Score (only if it clears the gates)

If no deal-breaker fired, score each dimension out of 10, then total out of 40:

- **Strategic** /10 — international client (strong plus), on-brand ("on TV"), showcase-worthy, portfolio fit
- **Commercial** /10 — margin, value-buyer, future work, worth the effort, acceptable risk
- **Delivery** /10 — technical capability, right PM or subcontractor available, timeline fit, easy trades/builder
- **Relationship** /10 — trust, realistic, decisive, real decision-maker, respects expertise, energises the team

| Total | Verdict |
|-------|---------|
| **36–40** | Chase aggressively |
| **30–35** | Good project — proceed |
| **24–29** | Proceed with caution (attach conditions) |
| **Below 24** | Decline |

A high score never overrides a deal-breaker. And a R4M job can still be a decline if everyone already
knows it will become a nightmare.

---

## Step 4 — Deliver the verdict (one glance)

Return a compact card, not an essay:

```
VERDICT: Pursue / Caution / Decline
Score:   XX/40   (S x · C x · D x · R x)
Why:     one line
Flags:   any deal-breakers or conditions
```

- **Pursue** → confirm and offer the next action (draft the reply / create the Asana lead / book a call).
- **Caution** → name the 1–2 conditions that would turn it into a yes (e.g. "only if payment terms move
  to 50% upfront").
- **Decline** → go to Step 5.

---

## Step 5 — Decline respectfully (protect the relationship)

Declining well is a power move, not a burnt bridge. Winelands architects, builders and clients all talk
to each other — a gracious no today is the referral or the better-fit project next year. So: be warm,
be brief, be honest without listing faults, and leave a clear door open. Where possible offer something
useful (a referral, a lighter-scope alternative, or "come back to us when…").

Draft a ready-to-send Gmail using the reason that actually applies. Pick the closest template and tailor
it — never send a template raw.

**Not the right fit (spec locked / can't specify kit / off-brand / scope mismatch):**
> Hi [name], thanks for the detail on [project]. Having looked it over, I don't think D-One is the right
> fit for this particular scope — we work best when we can specify and stand behind the core system, and
> I'd rather tell you that now than take it on halfway. Happy to point you toward someone better suited if
> that's useful, and I'd welcome the chance to quote your next project where we can do our best work.

**Price / value mismatch:**
> Hi [name], thank you for the opportunity on [project]. We've had a proper look and I don't think we're
> the right match on budget for this one — our value sits in reliability, design and long-term support,
> and I'd rather be honest than cut corners to hit a number. No hard feelings at all; if priorities shift
> or a future project has room for that approach, we'd love to be back in the conversation.

**Timing / genuinely can't take it on well right now:**
> Hi [name], thank you for thinking of D-One for [project] — it looks like a great one. In fairness to you,
> I'd rather be straight than overpromise on a project of this quality right now. If your timing has any
> flex I'd love to be considered, and either way please keep us in mind for the next one.

Principles across all three: no begging, no over-apologising, no bad-mouthing the client or competitors,
and always a genuine door left open.

---

## Fast path

If the enquiry already contains a confirmed deal-breaker (e.g. "please send your cheapest quote", margin
that can't reach 30%, or it's plainly outside the service area), skip straight to a **Decline** verdict and
offer the decline draft — no need to run the questions. Speed here is part of the discipline.
